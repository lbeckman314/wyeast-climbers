Skiers were getting hurt more and more often on the mountain, with no one to look after them. [The Mount Hood Ski Patrol dates its beginning to 1937](https://mthoodskipatrol.org/our-history/), when the Forest Service hired Wy'easter Hank Lewis for $10 a weekend to care for the injured and organize a formal patrol. Lewis brought an old toboggan from the Wy'east cabin and took a first-aid class in Portland. The patrol's history credits Wy'east president Everett Darr and Barney Macnab, captain of the Nile River Yacht Club, with pressing the Forest Service to act.

*To verify:* a correction we've received says it was Hank Lewis, not Everett Darr, who pushed for mountain rescue and the ski patrol, and that Darr became a prominent figure only later, after he opened his shop in Government Camp. The patrol's own history credits Darr and Macnab; Ric Conrad's book should settle it.
