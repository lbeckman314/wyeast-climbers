Skiers were getting hurt more and more often on the mountain, with no one to look after them. [The Mount Hood Ski Patrol dates its beginning to 1937](https://mthoodskipatrol.org/our-history/), when the Forest Service hired Wy'easter Hank Lewis for $10 a weekend to care for the injured and organize a formal patrol. Lewis brought an old toboggan from the Wy'east cabin and took a first-aid class in Portland. The patrol's history credits Barney Macnab, captain of the Nile River Yacht Club, and the Wy'east Climbers' president with pressing the Forest Service to act.

*To verify:* who pushed for mountain rescue and the ski patrol. A correction we've received credits Hank Lewis; the patrol's own history credits Macnab and the club's president. Ric Conrad's book should settle it.
