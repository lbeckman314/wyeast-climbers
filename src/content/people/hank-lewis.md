The Mount Hood Ski Patrol dates its beginning to 1937, when Hank Lewis, a Wy'east Climber (national patrol number 78), became the first patroller on the mountain. The Forest Service paid him $10 a weekend to look after injured skiers and organize a formal patrol. He brought his own equipment, including an old toboggan from the Wy'east cabin, and took a first-aid class in Portland. When the patrol incorporated on March 2, 1938, he became its patrol chief.

Decades later Lewis was one of Ric Conrad's key interviewees and was "instrumental," in Conrad's words, in reconstructing the club's rescue and recovery operations.
