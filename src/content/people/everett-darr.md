Everett Darr was a president of the Wy'east Climbers. The Mount Hood Ski Patrol's history names him, with Barney Macnab of the Nile River Yacht Club, as having pressed the U.S. Forest Service to look after injured skiers, but the patrol itself began with Wy'easter Hank Lewis, its first patroller and first patrol chief.

His American Alpine Journal obituary credits him, with James Mount and Ralph Calkin, with the first climb of the **Wy'east Route** on Mount Hood.

In 1937 Everett and his wife **Ida Darr** started the Mountain Shop in Portland. Darr later kept a Mountain Shop in Government Camp, where the early ski patrol based itself, and went on to run ski areas there, including Multorpor. Younger climbers and skiers knew him as "Papa Darr." He died on June 21, 1981, at 74.

*To verify:* a correction we've received says it was Hank Lewis, not Everett Darr, who pushed for mountain rescue and the ski patrol, and that Darr became a prominent figure only later, after he opened his shop in Government Camp. The patrol's own history credits Darr and Macnab; Ric Conrad's book should settle it.
